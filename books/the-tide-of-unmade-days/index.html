There is a street in Saltmere that no map admits to. It appears at low water, collects its rent from the living, and slides back under. And now the tide has begun delivering bills for a day that no one lived — a day the world was billed for and does not remember.

Restorer Cassia Merel has seen the impossible before; this time it has an address. With the dead man who answers doors, Elias Rooke, she follows the Row’s ledger down to the kept bell of Saltmere — drowned, chained, and scheduled to ring again at low water, 4:12, on the first of November: THE DAY LIVED.

The Trust wants the street. The tide wants its day. And the borough’s newest rule — we are a borough of record now — means whatever happens will be written down, countersigned, and kept. A day that does not die waits. And a waiting day requires an address.
